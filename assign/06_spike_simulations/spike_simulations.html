We can also add a refractory period to the neuron. This makes firing more regular and less Poisson-like.

neuron2 = StationaryPoissonProcess(rate=rate, t_start=t_start, t_stop=t_stop, refractory_period=2*pq.ms)
coefficient_of_variation = []
for i in range(1000):
    spike_train = neuron2.generate_spiketrain()
    inter_spike_intervals = isi(spike_train)
    coefficient_of_variation.append(cv(inter_spike_intervals))
plt.hist(coefficient_of_variation)
plt.axvline(x=1, ymin=0, ymax=1, linestyle='--', color="black")
plt.xlabel("Coefficient of Variation")
plt.ylabel("Number of Observations")
Text(0, 0.5, 'Number of Observations')

Often, neurons do not behave as poisson processes because their firing depends on perceptual, cognitive or motor events. We can simulate this as a NonStationaryPoissonProcess with a firing rate that varies over time. First, we generate the time-varying rate as an AnalogSignal.

t_start = 0 * pq.s
t_stop = 5 * pq.s
modulation_amplitude = 10 * pq.Hz
modulation_rate = 0.5 * pq.Hz
sampling_rate = 1000 * pq.Hz
duration = t_stop - t_start
n_samples = int(duration * sampling_rate)
t = np.linspace(t_start, t_stop, n_samples)
signal = modulation_amplitude*np.sin(2*np.pi*modulation_rate*t)
signal += modulation_amplitude
plt.plot(t, signal)

Create an AnalogSignal and use it as a time-varying rate_signal for the NonStationaryPoissonProcess

rate_signal = AnalogSignal(
    signal=signal,
    sampling_rate=sampling_rate,
    t_start=t_start,
    t_stop=t_stop,
    )
neuron3 = NonStationaryPoissonProcess(rate_signal=rate_signal)

Plot the modulation frequency together with the spike times

fig, ax = plt.subplots(2, sharex=True)
spike_times = neuron3.generate_spiketrain().times
ax[0].plot(rate_signal.times, rate_signal)
ax[1].eventplot(spike_times)
